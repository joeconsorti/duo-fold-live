# Alpha.89 · Lighter in-app folds

Home and regular apps share the cached frosted preview and its 120 ms dissolve. On a regular app fold the outgoing screenshot keeps its original proportions. Duo crops it once into a full-panel texture up to 24 pixels on the long edge. The app preview uses one filtered texture lookup for the glass, including poses that project past the hinge. The native-resolution glass settings take over when the destination provides an accepted app image. A fold that already has a matching destination image skips the frost. The endpoint can end the cosmetic dissolve immediately.

On the direct app route, Duo keeps the preview until Android reports stable geometry on the new primary. In the earlier alpha.86 YouTube export, the 12 retained secondary-display capture attempts all returned no image. Alpha.89 removes that speculative request. An accepted primary capture can provide the first time-stamped window observation, followed by independent confirmation. Native reveal still requires the correct task, physical panel, rotation, powered geometry and a committed destination frame. Apps still need their task moved to the destination. Home uses Android’s launcher placement.

The app capture now follows the render resolution selected at session start, including supersampling. At 25% with standard antialiasing on an SM-F971U, the long edge is 493 pixels on the cover and 612 on the inner screen, compared with 1,440 in alpha.88. The full-quality ceiling stays at 1,440. Blur scales with the capture so its radius in panel coordinates stays consistent. Existing owner, opacity, competing-window, protected-content and brightness checks remain in the handoff.

## Handset check

Compare the same app, brightness, starting thermal status and fold cadence on alpha.88 and alpha.89. Leave the optional image sequence off during a thermal comparison. Check slow and quick folds in both directions, a pause, and a reversal. Use physical observations for visible continuity and the diagnostic fold history for per-fold app CPU time, selected resolution, thermal status and battery-sensor temperature. The software-only tests cannot measure heat on the handset.
